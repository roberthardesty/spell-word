### A Reading Companion That Fits in a Pocket

Learning to read is full of small moments where everything stalls. A child is making it through a story, hits a word they don't know, and the spell breaks. They need a parent, a teacher, or a dictionary and most of the time, none of those are within reach.

**The product**

A small, battery-powered device that clips to any book. When a young reader gets stuck, they press a single button and spell the word out loud, letter by letter. The device figures out what word they meant and reads it back to them.

No screen. No app. No internet connection. One button, a microphone, a speaker, and a voice that helps when help is needed.

**Why it matters**

Early reading fluency is one of the strongest predictors of long-term academic outcomes, and the biggest enemy of fluency is friction — every unknown word is a chance to disengage. Existing tools either require expensive hardware (reading pens with cameras), pull kids onto screens (tablet apps), or assume an adult is always nearby (smart speakers). This device is the opposite: cheap, private, child-operable, and meant to live alongside whatever physical book a child happens to be holding.

**How it works**

Inside is a small microcontroller running a neural network trained to recognize spoken letters. When the child spells a word aloud, the device matches their letters against the 3,000 most common words in English and plays a pre-recorded pronunciation. All processing happens on-device — no audio is uploaded, no account is required, no data leaves the unit.

**Who it's for**

Early readers and struggling readers, roughly ages 5–10. ESL learners building vocabulary. Parents who want a screen-free literacy tool. Classrooms and libraries where one tablet per child isn't realistic but books are everywhere.

**The bet**

That a single-purpose, physical, `$25-ish` dollar object beats a `$500` tablet running a $10 app — because the limitation *is* the feature. The device only does one thing, so a child can use it without help, and a parent can hand it over without worry.
